The net energy of a wind farm is the gross energy minus losses. Typical loss categories are: wake losses (5 to 15 percent depending on layout and spacing), turbine availability (about 2 to 4 percent), electrical losses in cables and transformers (1.5 to 3 percent), grid availability and curtailment, environmental losses such as blade icing or degradation, and turbine performance losses when the real power curve is below the warranted one.

Availability is the fraction of time the turbines are ready to generate. A time-based availability of 97 percent means turbines were available 97 percent of the hours. Energy-based availability is more useful because downtime during windy periods costs more energy than downtime in calm periods.

Losses are multiplied, not added: net = gross x (1 - wake) x (1 - availability) x (1 - electrical) and so on. Statistical tools such as Markov chain models can simulate turbine failure and repair sequences to estimate realistic availability losses and their variability.
